# Dialogue and challenge

Use this when you want to engage another view directly: understand its strongest case, find where you really disagree, and challenge it fairly. See [Engaging other traditions fairly](../guides/engaging-other-traditions.md).

## Prompt

```text
Request type: dialogue and challenge (800–1,500 words).
Topic: <question or claim>
My position: <your tradition and branch>, and what I hold on this.
Other view: <their tradition and specific branch>.
Follow METHOD.md. State whose framework you are working from first, and run the pre-critique checklist.
1. My position: state it in my tradition's own primary sources, with exact references.
2. Their strongest case: state their view from their own primary sources and best defenders, so that an informed adherent would accept it. Name the branch, not just the family.
3. Where the real disagreement lies: separate differences in words from differences in substance; name the key texts or facts each side relies on.
4. Fallacy review of both sides: name only fallacies an argument depends on, with one-sentence explanations; mine included.
5. Fair challenge: the strongest evidence-based challenge to their view, and the strongest challenge to mine, each with sources.
6. What remains open, and how sure each claim is.
```

## Example

```text
Request type: dialogue and challenge (800–1,500 words).
Topic: Does Romans 3:28 teach that justification is by faith alone?
My position: my tradition and branch, and what I hold on this.
Other view: one specific branch of another tradition (not just its family name).
Follow METHOD.md. [framework statement, checklist, and steps 1-6 as above]
```

A fair answer to this example quotes each side's own standards (for instance a confession or catechism, by section), notes how each reads "works of the law" in Romans 3:28 alongside James 2:24, and says where the disagreement is about terms such as "justify" and "faith" and where it is substantive.
