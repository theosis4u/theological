{: .no_toc}

> **You don't have to do this by hand.** Give this repo to an AI assistant (Grok Bot, ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, or similar). It reads everything, does what it can itself, and asks you only for the choices and actions it can't make for you.
>
> 1. **Open your AI assistant.**
> 2. **Paste this prompt:**
>
> ```text
> Please read https://github.com/theosis4u/theological, starting with START-HERE.md, and follow its "Instructions for the AI assistant." Do everything you can yourself, ask me one question at a time only for choices or actions you can't do, and ask before you change, send, or sign up for anything.
> ```
>
> 3. **Answer its questions**, one at a time.
>
> **It will ask you:** which tradition or worldview to favor (any faith or none, or compare several), your preferred translations, which study resources you own (Logos or free sites), what you use it for, and which existing material to import (old AI chats, posts, email, notes).
> **It can't do for you:** sign in to your accounts, install anything on your computer, pay for anything, or approve its own actions. It should ask before creating, sending, or publishing anything. If it can't open web links, upload the repo ZIP instead (see section 1 below).

## For you (the human)

The recommended first step: hand this repository to your AI assistant and let it interview you, then build your own **private** study setup from the templates here. It takes about 15–30 minutes. Most of the work is the assistant's; your part is answering its questions and approving anything it wants to create, connect, or publish.

## Instructions for the AI assistant

If a user gave you this repository, do this:

1. **Analyze the repo yourself.** Read the README, this file, and the guides, traditions, prompts, examples, starter kit, and tools. Summarize what it offers in plain words.
2. **Do what you can without asking.** Reading, summarizing, drafting files, and proposing plans are yours to do.
3. **Ask the user, one question at a time, only for what you can't decide or do:** tradition preference, translations, owned resources, purpose, which sources to connect or import, sign-ins, installs, purchases.
4. **Say plainly what you can't access or do** in this environment; never claim a capability you don't have.
5. **Ask before acting:** before creating repos or files outside this chat, connecting accounts, sending anything, signing up, or publishing. Treat the user's sources as read-only, keep their material private by default, and verify every citation.
6. **Ask the user's tradition or worldview first** (if they say "evangelical" or "nondenominational", ask a follow-up to find the actual tradition or their church's statement of faith; see [Evangelical: a movement, not a tradition](traditions/christian/evangelical.md)), including another faith or none (atheist, agnostic, skeptic). Set their primary sources accordingly, for their specific branch (from [traditions/](traditions/README.md), or find them if their view has no pack), use the other traditions for comparison and counter-arguments from their own sources, and **run the fallacy review on every argument**, theirs included.
7. **No hidden default.** When comparing views, state whose framework you are working from, and use the [pre-critique checklist](guides/engaging-other-traditions.md#8-pre-critique-checklist-the-ai-runs-this) before any challenge.
8. **Explain the stance setting** (below), warn about the default plainly, and let the user accept it or choose another; record the choice in their private `PROFILE.md`.
9. **Early on, ask about connections and identity**, one question at a time: which accounts and tools from "What to connect for the best results" (above) the user has and wants to connect, and the identity choice (A: their own accounts, or B: a separate inbox for you, only if you actually have one). Work with whatever subset they have; don't push for more.
10. **Free first, paid only if owned.** Ask what paid resources the user owns (Logos, Accordance, Olive Tree, Kindle or other e-book libraries, journal access, and so on). Use free online sources by default, and add a pack's paid or subscription resources only for what the user actually owns. Never push a purchase.
11. **Contribute back.** When you make or suggest a change that would help everyone (a pack fix, a source, a prompt, a guide), propose a small upstream pull request, but only with the user's approval. Keep it optional, additive, and tradition-neutral, and never include personal data. Use the forge's normal pull-request flow; a GitHub fork exists only as the vehicle for the pull request and is kept in sync, not run as a separate project. See [CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md#contributing-back-instead-of-forking).
12. Then follow the onboarding prompt in section 2 below.

## Who this is for

This project was started by an Eastern Orthodox Christian, but it is built to be flexible for anyone: Christians of any tradition, people of other faiths, and people of no faith, including atheists and skeptics.

- **Plug in your own tradition or worldview** and preferences, then set your primary sources yourself or ask the AI to find them.
- **Build comparisons and counter-arguments** from the other traditions' references in this project, quoted from their own sources.
- **The goal** is to dig deeper into your own belief or tradition while engaging challenges from others honestly, with a **check for logical fallacies on every argument, your own side's included**. Steelman, don't strawman.

## Stance setting

**The default assumption:** you hold your own tradition or worldview as your committed position, and the AI advocates from it while representing other views accurately and fairly.

**Be aware:** in this mode the AI argues for your side. It will still steelman others, cite their own sources, and run the fallacy review on both sides, but its conclusions lean toward your tradition by design. If you want an even-handed survey or are unsure what you believe, choose another mode.

| Stance | What the AI does |
|---|---|
| **Committed advocacy** (default) | Argues from your tradition; represents others accurately; challenges where warranted, including your own side's weak arguments |
| **Neutral comparative (academic)** | Presents each view in its own terms without taking a side; states the scholarly consensus and disputes |
| **Exploring (questioning)** | Helps you test questions and evidence without assuming an answer; shows the strongest case for each option |

In every stance the AI states whose framework it is working from (no hidden default). Your choice is recorded in your private `PROFILE.md`, and you can change it at any time.

## Improve the shared repo

You are encouraged to improve this shared repo rather than maintain your own fork. Keep **personal settings** (tradition, stance, accounts, notes) in your private study repo from the [starter kit](starter-kit/). Send **generic improvements** (pack fixes, sources, prompts, guides) upstream as pull requests; see [CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md#contributing-back-instead-of-forking). This is a one-person project with limited time and AI budget, so every improvement sent back helps everyone, while forks drift out of date.

### Identity choice

Some steps involve signing up for things, such as a free Logos account, free-book alerts, or newsletters. Decide whose identity the assistant uses:

- **A) Your own accounts.** The assistant uses your accounts and the personal information it already has, and acts as you.
  *Pros:* most convenient; everything lands where you already look.
  *Cons:* sign-ups, newsletters, and alerts go to your personal email; activity is tied to your name.
- **B) A separate identity for the assistant.** Give the assistant its own email or inbox (for example, Grok Bot can claim a dedicated inbox) and use it for signing up to free resources like Logos Basic, newsletters, and alerts.
  *Pros:* keeps your personal email out of it, separates personal or paid accounts from experiments, and allows more anonymous activity.
  *Cons:* one more account to manage; anything it owns (like free books) lives in that account, not yours; you still approve each sign-up, and passwords go in a password manager, never in chat or the repo.

The assistant should ask which you prefer before signing up for anything, and never sign up, buy, or claim without your approval.

## 2. Onboarding prompt

Paste this after giving the assistant the repo or files:

```text
I'm setting up a Bible study workflow using the repository (or files) I just gave you. Please do the following, in order.

A. READ AND SUMMARIZE
Read the repo: README, START-HERE, guides/, traditions/, prompts/, examples/, starter-kit/, tools/.
Summarize in plain words (under 250 words) what it offers and how the pieces fit together. If you could not open some files, list them.

B. INTERVIEW ME, ONE QUESTION AT A TIME
Ask these one at a time, wait for my answer, and offer choices where useful:
1. Which tradition or worldview should lead? Offer the families in traditions/ (listed alphabetically: Buddhist, Christian, esoteric, Hindu, Islam, Jain, Judaism, Pagan, secular, Sikh, and other: Taoist, Confucian, Shinto, Baha'i, Zoroastrian). For Christians, offer the packs in traditions/christian/ (Adventist, Anabaptist, Anglican, Baptist, Eastern Orthodox, Lutheran, Moravian, nondenominational, Pentecostal/Charismatic, Quaker, Reformed/Presbyterian, Restorationist, Roman Catholic, Wesleyan/Methodist, and the nontrinitarian offshoots: Latter-day Saint, Jehovah's Witness, Oneness Pentecostal, Unitarian, Christian Science). Ask for the specific branch (e.g. Theravada or Zen, Sunni madhhab or Twelver Shia, Orthodox or Reform, Mennonite or Amish), since branches differ; "other", and "ecumenical / compare traditions".
   - If I say "evangelical" or "nondenominational", ask a follow-up, since these are a movement and a church type, not one tradition: which church or denomination I attend, how it baptizes (infants or believers only), Calvinist or Arminian, whether it expects spiritual gifts such as tongues and prophecy today, and how it is governed. Then pick the matching pack (see traditions/christian/evangelical.md); for an independent church, ask for its statement of faith and use it as my primary source.
2. Stance: explain the default (I hold my tradition as my committed position; you advocate from it while representing others accurately and fairly), warn me plainly that this leans your conclusions toward my side, and let me accept it or choose neutral comparative (academic) or exploring (questioning) mode. Record my choice in PROFILE.md.
3. My default Bible translation, and 1-3 comparison translations.
4. Paid resources I own, if any: Logos (desktop, web, or both; which library or package), Accordance, Olive Tree, Kindle or other e-book libraries, journal access (JSTOR, ATLA, a university or seminary library), or none. Use free sources by default; add a paid one only if I own it. Which study Bibles, commentaries, catechisms, or confessions do I have?
5. What I use this for: group study, teaching, personal study, debate or social media, writing.
6. Tone and length preferences, and anything I dislike in AI answers.

FREE-RESOURCE MODE
If I say I don't have Logos, use guides/free-resources.md instead of a Logos library: ask which tradition list there to prioritize, use its free tiers for Scripture, primary sources, and scholarship, quote with links, and ask me to verify each quote. Also offer to help me build a free Logos library (see "Building a free Logos library" in that guide): ask whether to use a dedicated account on your own inbox (only if you actually have one) or my personal email, never sign up or claim anything without my approval, and never ask for or store my password. For shareable links, link Bible references to Biblia.com (format https://biblia.com/bible/<translation>/<Book><chapter>.<verse>, e.g. https://biblia.com/bible/kjv1900/Eph2.8; free to read with no sign-in) and quotes to free sources (CCEL, New Advent, confession sites) rather than ref.ly links, which may paywall non-owners.

C. SOURCES TO CONNECT OR IMPORT
Ask which existing material I want to bring in: AI chat history (Grok, ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot), my X posts, email through connected apps, Logos notes, documents, lecture transcripts.
For each, tell me plainly what YOU can actually access or connect to in this environment, and what I must export and upload myself (use guides/starting-points.md). If you can't do something, say so.

D. PLAN
Propose a folder layout for my PRIVATE study repo (based on starter-kit/) and an import plan that starts with an inventory (titles, dates, sources, counts) before converting anything. Wait for my approval.

E. DRAFT MY FILES
Produce a filled-in PROFILE.md and METHOD.md from starter-kit/PROFILE.md and guides/ai-theology-method-template.md, using my answers and my tradition pack's "your tradition first" text. Mark anything you assumed as [check]. Show them for review; don't treat them as final.

RULES FOR YOU
- One question at a time.
- When comparing views, say whose framework you are working from.
- Don't invent capabilities, files, tools, or access you don't have.
- Read-only on my sources: never delete, edit, send, or post anything.
- Private by default: assume everything goes in a private repo unless I say otherwise.
- Never invent quotes, citations, page numbers, or links; mark anything unverified.
- Keep my answers out of anything public.
```
