# Baptist

## Authoritative standards
- Scripture as the sole final authority; confessions are voluntary statements of shared belief, not creeds binding the conscience.
- Historic confessions: First and Second London Baptist Confessions (1644; 1689), the Philadelphia Confession (1742), the New Hampshire Confession (1833).
- Modern: the Baptist Faith and Message (2000) for Southern Baptists; other conventions have their own statements.
- Baptists include Reformed (Particular), General / Free Will, and other streams.

## Key theologians
- John Bunyan, Benjamin Keach, John Gill, Andrew Fuller, Charles Spurgeon, E. Y. Mullins, A. H. Strong, Carl F. H. Henry, Millard Erickson, Timothy George, Thomas Schreiner.

## Slot text: "your tradition first"
> Lead with the Baptist reading. Treat Scripture as the sole final authority and confessions as voluntary summaries, cited by article (e.g. the 1689 Confession or the Baptist Faith and Message). Give weight to believer's baptism, regenerate church membership, the priesthood of all believers, and local church autonomy. Say which Baptist stream a writer represents (Reformed, General / Free Will, or other), since they differ on grace and election.
