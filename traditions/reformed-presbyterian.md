# Reformed / Presbyterian

## Authoritative standards
- Scripture as the only infallible rule (*sola Scriptura*), with confessions as subordinate standards.
- The Westminster Standards (1647): Confession of Faith, Larger Catechism, Shorter Catechism. Cite as WCF 3.1, WLC Q. 1, WSC Q. 1.
- The Three Forms of Unity: Belgic Confession (1561), Heidelberg Catechism (1563), Canons of Dort (1618–19), including the Rejections of Errors.
- Also: Second Helvetic Confession, Scots Confession.

## Key theologians
- Reformation and after: John Calvin, Heinrich Bullinger, Zacharias Ursinus, Francis Turretin, John Owen, Jonathan Edwards.
- Modern: Charles Hodge, B. B. Warfield, Herman Bavinck, Geerhardus Vos, Louis Berkhof, John Murray, R. C. Sproul, Michael Horton, Richard Muller.

## Study Bibles and Logos resources
- *Reformation Study Bible* (Ligonier).
- *ESV Study Bible* (Crossway), widely used though not exclusively Reformed.
- Calvin's *Institutes* and *Commentaries*.
- Bavinck, *Reformed Dogmatics* _(verify in Logos)_.
- Historical creeds and confessions collections (e.g. Schaff, *The Creeds of Christendom*).

## Translation preferences
ESV and NASB are common; NKJV and KJV in some churches; CSB in others. Attention to the original languages is expected.

## Slot text: "your tradition first"
> Lead with the Reformed reading. Treat Scripture as the only infallible rule and interpret Scripture by Scripture. Quote the confessional standards (the Westminster Standards or the Three Forms of Unity) by section, and treat them as faithful summaries subordinate to Scripture. Frame the argument through covenant theology and God's sovereign grace. Distinguish confessional doctrine from the views of individual Reformed theologians and from intramural debates.
