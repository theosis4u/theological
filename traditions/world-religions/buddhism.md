## Overview

Buddhists take refuge in the Three Jewels: the Buddha (the awakened teacher, Siddhartha Gautama), the Dharma (his teaching), and the Sangha (the community). Common ground across schools includes the Four Noble Truths, the Noble Eightfold Path, the three marks of existence (impermanence, unsatisfactoriness, non-self), karma and rebirth, and nirvana as liberation from suffering. Schools differ on which scriptures are authoritative, the ideal (arhat or bodhisattva), and the methods of practice.

## Branches at a glance

| | Theravada | Mahayana | Vajrayana |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where | Sri Lanka, Thailand, Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia | China, Japan, Korea, Vietnam | Tibet, Bhutan, Mongolia, Himalaya |
| Canon | Pali Canon (Tipitaka) | Chinese canon; Mahayana sutras added | Tibetan Kangyur and Tengyur |
| Ideal | Arhat (liberated disciple) | Bodhisattva (awakening for all beings) | Bodhisattva, with tantric methods |
| Distinctive | Early discourses, Abhidhamma, monastic Vinaya | Emptiness, Buddha-nature, many Buddhas and bodhisattvas | Guru, empowerment, mantra, visualization |

## Theravada

- **Distinctive beliefs:** follows the Pali Canon as the oldest complete record of the Buddha's teaching; emphasizes the arhat path, meditation (samatha and vipassana), and monastic discipline. Generally does not accept the later Mahayana sutras as the Buddha's word.
- **Key texts and authorities:** the Tipitaka (Vinaya, Sutta, and Abhidhamma Pitakas); Buddhaghosa's *Visuddhimagga*; senior monastics of the national Sanghas.
- **Free sources:** [SuttaCentral](https://suttacentral.net/) (early texts with translations), [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/), [dhammatalks.org](https://www.dhammatalks.org/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Theravada reading. Cite the Pali Canon by collection and sutta number (e.g. SN 56.11) and the commentarial tradition where relevant. Distinguish the early discourses from later commentary, and note where Mahayana schools read the same theme differently.*

## Mahayana

- **Distinctive beliefs:** the bodhisattva ideal of awakening for the sake of all beings; emptiness (sunyata) of all phenomena; Buddha-nature; accepts Mahayana sutras (e.g. the Lotus, Heart, and Diamond Sutras) as authoritative.
- **Key texts and authorities:** Nagarjuna (Madhyamaka) and Asanga and Vasubandhu (Yogacara); the Chinese Buddhist canon; founders and patriarchs of each school.
- **Free sources:** [CBETA Online](https://cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw/) (Chinese canon), [SuttaCentral](https://suttacentral.net/) (also has Chinese parallels), [sacred-texts.com: Buddhism](https://sacred-texts.com/bud/index.htm) (older translations; check the edition).

### Zen (Chan, Seon, Thien)
- **Distinctive:** direct insight through meditation (zazen) and, in Rinzai, koan practice; transmission "outside the scriptures"; lineage of teachers. Soto and Rinzai differ in emphasis (silent illumination vs. koan).
- **Texts and authorities:** the Platform Sutra, Dogen's *Shobogenzo* (Soto), koan collections (*Gateless Gate*, *Blue Cliff Record*).
- **Free:** [Soto Zen Buddhism (Sotoshu)](https://www.sotozen.com/eng/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Zen reading of the named school (Soto or Rinzai). Cite Dogen, the Platform Sutra, or the koan collections by case, and distinguish practice instructions from doctrinal claims.*

### Pure Land
- **Distinctive:** reliance on the vow of Amitabha Buddha and recitation of his name (nembutsu) for rebirth in the Pure Land; Jodo Shinshu stresses entrusting faith over self-power practice.
- **Texts and authorities:** the three Pure Land sutras; Honen (Jodo-shu), Shinran (Jodo Shinshu).
- **Free:** [Jodo Shu North America](https://www.jodoshuna.org/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Pure Land reading of the named school (Jodo-shu or Jodo Shinshu), citing the Pure Land sutras and Honen or Shinran. Note the self-power / other-power distinction where relevant.*

### Tiantai / Tendai and Nichiren
- **Distinctive:** the Lotus Sutra as the supreme teaching. Tiantai/Tendai systematizes all teachings around it; Nichiren schools focus on chanting its title (daimoku). Nichiren schools (including Nichiren Shu, Nichiren Shoshu, and lay movements such as Soka Gakkai) disagree with each other on authority and lineage.
- **Texts and authorities:** the Lotus Sutra; Zhiyi (Tiantai); Nichiren's writings (gosho).
- **Free:** [Nichiren Buddhism Library](https://www.nichirenlibrary.org/) (Soka Gakkai translations).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the reading of the named Lotus school (Tendai, Nichiren Shu, Nichiren Shoshu, or Soka Gakkai), citing the Lotus Sutra by chapter and Nichiren's writings by title. Say which organization's interpretation you are using.*

## Vajrayana (Tibetan Buddhism)

- **Distinctive beliefs:** Mahayana philosophy plus tantric practice: initiation (empowerment) by a qualified guru, mantra, deity visualization, and esoteric yogas, seen as a swifter path. Schools: Nyingma (oldest; Dzogchen), Kagyu (Mahamudra), Sakya (Lamdre), Gelug (scholastic; the Dalai Lama's school), with Jonang as a smaller school.
- **Key texts and authorities:** the Kangyur and Tengyur; Tsongkhapa (Gelug), Longchenpa (Nyingma), Milarepa and Gampopa (Kagyu); living lineage heads.
- **Free sources:** [84000](https://84000.co/) (translations of the Kangyur and Tengyur), [Rigpa Wiki](https://www.rigpawiki.org/) (reference, Nyingma-leaning).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Tibetan Buddhist reading of the named school (Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, or Gelug). Cite the Kangyur/Tengyur text or the school's masters, and note where tantric teachings are restricted to initiates rather than describing them in detail.*

## Primary vs comparison sources

- **Primary:** your branch's canon and its recognized teachers.
- **Comparison:** the other branches' canons (e.g. Pali parallels to Mahayana sutras), academic Buddhist studies, and other religions' views on self, suffering, and liberation.

## Points of dialogue

- Non-self (anatta) vs. the soul in theistic religions; rebirth vs. resurrection or a single life.
- Whether ultimate reality is personal (theism) or not; grace in Pure Land compared with grace in Christianity.
- Meditation and contemplative prayer; ethics without a creator God.
