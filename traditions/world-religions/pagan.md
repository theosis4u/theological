## Overview

Modern Paganism is a family of religions inspired by pre-Christian traditions, often nature-centered and polytheistic or pantheistic, with ritual marking seasons and life passages. There is no single scripture or central authority. A key divide is between **reconstructionist** traditions, which try to revive a specific historical religion as faithfully as sources allow, and **eclectic** or revival traditions, which draw freely on many sources and personal experience.

## Branches at a glance

| | Wicca | Heathenry | Hellenism | Druidry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inspiration | 20th-century revival (Gerald Gardner) drawing on folklore and ceremonial magic | Pre-Christian Norse and Germanic religion | Ancient Greek religion | Celtic culture and the 18th-century Druid revival |
| Approach | Revival; initiatory covens and solitary practice | Mostly reconstructionist | Reconstructionist | Revival; often eclectic |
| Deities | Often a Goddess and God; varies | Norse/Germanic gods (Odin, Thor, Frigg, etc.) | Olympian and other Greek gods | Varies (Celtic deities, nature, or non-theistic) |
| Sources | Gardnerian and Alexandrian Books of Shadows; modern authors | Eddas, sagas, archaeology | Homer, Hesiod, Orphic hymns, classical sources | Medieval Welsh and Irish literature; revival writings |

## Wicca

- **Distinctive beliefs:** a nature religion honoring the Goddess and God; the Wheel of the Year (eight sabbats); ritual magic; the ethic "An it harm none, do what ye will." Traditional (Gardnerian, Alexandrian) covens are initiatory; many practitioners are eclectic or solitary.
- **Key texts and authorities:** Gerald Gardner and Doreen Valiente; tradition-specific Books of Shadows (largely private); no central authority.
- **Free sources:** [The Pagan Federation](https://www.paganfed.org/) (UK umbrella body; overviews), [sacred-texts.com: Paganism](https://sacred-texts.com/pag/index.htm).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Wiccan reading of the named tradition (Gardnerian, Alexandrian, or eclectic). Distinguish historical claims from revival practice, and don't present private initiatory material.*

## Heathenry

- **Distinctive beliefs:** revival of Norse and Germanic religion: the gods and other beings, reciprocity (gift for a gift), honor, ancestors, and community (kindreds). Most Heathen organizations explicitly reject racist interpretations, which some fringe groups promote.
- **Key texts and authorities:** the Poetic and Prose Eddas, the sagas, archaeology; no central authority.
- **Free sources:** [The Troth](https://www.thetroth.org/) (inclusive Heathen organization), [sacred-texts.com: Germanic and Norse texts](https://sacred-texts.com/neu/index.htm) (older translations; check the edition).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Heathen reading, citing the Eddas and sagas by name and section, distinguishing historical evidence from modern practice, and following inclusive Heathen organizations.*

## Hellenism (Hellenic polytheism)

- **Distinctive beliefs:** reverence for the Greek gods through offerings, festivals, and virtues such as hospitality (xenia) and piety (eusebeia); mostly reconstructionist.
- **Key texts and authorities:** Homer, Hesiod, the Homeric and Orphic Hymns, classical philosophers and historians.
- **Free sources:** [Theoi Project](https://www.theoi.com/) (Greek mythology with ancient sources), [Perseus Digital Library](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/) (Greek texts), [Hellenion](https://www.hellenion.org/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Hellenic polytheist reading, citing ancient authors by work and line, and distinguishing ancient practice from modern reconstruction.*

## Druidry

- **Distinctive beliefs:** a spiritual path inspired by the ancient Celtic druids as transmitted through medieval literature and the 18th-century revival: nature, creativity (the bardic arts), and wisdom. Members range from polytheist to animist to non-theist.
- **Key texts and authorities:** medieval Welsh and Irish literature (e.g. the *Mabinogion*); revival and modern orders such as the Order of Bards, Ovates and Druids.
- **Free sources:** [Order of Bards, Ovates and Druids](https://druidry.org/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Druid reading of the named order, distinguishing what is known about ancient druids from revival and modern practice.*

## Primary vs comparison sources

- **Primary:** your tradition's sources and recognized elders or organizations.
- **Comparison:** academic history of ancient religion (to separate evidence from revival), other Pagan traditions, and other religions' views of nature, divinity, and ritual.

## Points of dialogue

- Polytheism, pantheism, and monotheism; the sacredness of nature.
- Christianization of Europe and its historical record; continuity vs. revival.
- Ethics without a single revealed law.
