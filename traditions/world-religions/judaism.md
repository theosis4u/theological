## Overview

Judaism is the covenant faith and way of life of the Jewish people, grounded in the Torah (in its widest sense, the written Torah and the oral tradition), centered on one God, the commandments (mitzvot), study, prayer, and the life of the community. Movements differ on the authority of halakha (Jewish law), how revelation is understood, and how tradition adapts to modern life.

## Branches at a glance

| | Orthodox | Conservative (Masorti) | Reform | Reconstructionist |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revelation | Torah given by God at Sinai, written and oral | Divine revelation understood through history and scholarship | Progressive revelation; human authorship acknowledged | Judaism as an evolving religious civilization |
| Halakha | Binding; decided by rabbinic authorities | Binding but evolving, through its law committee | Not binding; informed personal choice | A guide, decided by community values |
| Practice | Traditional observance; separate seating in prayer | Traditional with adaptations (e.g. egalitarian in most congregations) | Liberal; egalitarian | Egalitarian; community-led |

## Orthodox Judaism

- **Distinctive beliefs:** the written and oral Torah are divine and binding; halakha as codified in the Talmud and later codes governs life.
- **Haredi (including Hasidic):** strict observance and separation from secular culture; Hasidic groups follow a Rebbe and emphasize joyful devotion and mysticism (e.g. Chabad, Satmar, Ger). Slot text: *Lead with the Haredi or named Hasidic reading, citing the Talmud, codes (Shulchan Aruch), and the group's Rebbes or sages.*
- **Modern Orthodox:** full observance combined with engagement in secular education and society. Slot text: *Lead with the Modern Orthodox reading, citing halakhic sources and recognized Modern Orthodox authorities.*
- **Key texts and authorities:** Tanakh, Mishnah, Talmud, Maimonides' *Mishneh Torah*, the *Shulchan Aruch*; leading rabbinic decisors.
- **Free sources:** [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/) (texts with linked commentaries), [Chabad.org](https://www.chabad.org/) (Hasidic, Chabad; blocks some automated fetches), [OU.org](https://www.ou.org/) (Modern Orthodox).

## Conservative (Masorti) Judaism

- **Distinctive beliefs:** halakha remains binding but develops historically; uses critical scholarship alongside tradition.
- **Key texts and authorities:** the classical texts; responsa of the Rabbinical Assembly's Committee on Jewish Law and Standards.
- **Free sources:** [Rabbinical Assembly](https://www.rabbinicalassembly.org/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Conservative/Masorti reading, citing classical sources and the Committee on Jewish Law and Standards' responsa, and note how it balances tradition and change.*

## Reform Judaism

- **Distinctive beliefs:** ethical monotheism and prophetic justice at the center; halakha guides but does not bind; individual autonomy informed by tradition.
- **Key texts and authorities:** Tanakh and rabbinic literature read critically; the movement's platforms (e.g. Pittsburgh 1885, Pittsburgh 1999).
- **Free sources:** [ReformJudaism.org](https://www.reformjudaism.org/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Reform reading, citing Tanakh and rabbinic sources alongside the movement's platforms, and distinguish ethical principles from ritual practice.*

## Reconstructionist Judaism

- **Distinctive beliefs:** founded on Mordecai Kaplan's view of Judaism as an evolving religious civilization; tradition has "a vote, not a veto"; many understand God non-supernaturally.
- **Free sources:** [Reconstructing Judaism](https://www.reconstructingjudaism.org/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Reconstructionist reading, citing Kaplan and the movement's sources, and note how it reinterprets traditional concepts.*

## Primary vs comparison sources

- **Primary:** Tanakh and rabbinic literature as read by your movement and its authorities.
- **Comparison:** other movements' responsa, academic Jewish studies, [Jewish Virtual Library](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/) and [My Jewish Learning](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/) for overviews, and Christian and Muslim readings of shared texts, quoted from their own sources.

## Points of dialogue

- Reading the Hebrew Bible: Jewish interpretation vs. Christian readings of the "Old Testament"; messianic texts.
- Law and grace; covenant and election; the relationship of Judaism and Christianity in history.
- Prophets and shared figures with Islam.
