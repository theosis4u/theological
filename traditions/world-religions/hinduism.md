## Overview

Many Hindus call their tradition Sanatana Dharma, the "eternal order." It is a family of traditions sharing the authority of the Vedas (in most streams), concepts of dharma, karma, samsara (rebirth), and moksha (liberation), and devotion to God or the divine in many forms. Branches differ on which deity is supreme, the relationship between the soul (atman) and ultimate reality (Brahman), and the path to liberation (knowledge, devotion, action, meditation).

## Branches at a glance

| | Vaishnava | Shaiva | Shakta | Smarta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supreme | Vishnu and his avatars (Krishna, Rama) | Shiva | The Goddess (Devi/Shakti) | Brahman, worshipped through five deities |
| Key texts | Bhagavad Gita, Bhagavata Purana, Pancharatra | Shaiva Agamas, Shiva Purana | Devi Mahatmya, Tantras | Vedas, Upanishads, Smritis |
| Typical philosophy | Vishishtadvaita, Dvaita, Achintya-bhedabheda | Shaiva Siddhanta, Kashmir Shaivism | Shakta Tantra | Advaita Vedanta |

| Vedanta school | Founder | Soul and Brahman |
|---|---|---|
| Advaita | Shankara | Non-dual: atman is Brahman; the world's multiplicity is ultimately illusory (maya) |
| Vishishtadvaita | Ramanuja | Qualified non-dualism: souls and world are real, the "body" of God |
| Dvaita | Madhva | Dualism: God, souls, and matter are eternally distinct |

## Vaishnava

- **Distinctive beliefs:** Vishnu (or Krishna as the supreme form, in Gaudiya Vaishnavism) is the Supreme Person; liberation through loving devotion (bhakti) and grace. Major sampradayas include Sri (Ramanuja), Madhva, Gaudiya (Chaitanya; ISKCON is a modern Gaudiya movement), and Pushti Marg (Vallabha).
- **Key texts and authorities:** Bhagavad Gita, Bhagavata Purana, Ramayana, the Alvars' hymns; acharyas Ramanuja, Madhva, Chaitanya, Vallabha.
- **Free sources:** [Vedabase](https://vedabase.io/) (ISKCON translations of the Gita and Bhagavata), [Gita Supersite (IIT Kanpur)](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/) (the Gita with several classical commentaries).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Vaishnava reading of the named sampradaya (Sri, Madhva, Gaudiya, or Pushti). Cite the Gita by chapter and verse and the Bhagavata Purana by canto, chapter, and verse, and name the acharya whose commentary you follow.*

## Shaiva

- **Distinctive beliefs:** Shiva as the Supreme; grace liberates the soul from bonds. Shaiva Siddhanta (South India) holds souls distinct from Shiva; Kashmir Shaivism is non-dual (recognition of one's identity with Shiva); Lingayats (Virashaiva) reject caste and temple ritual in favor of personal devotion.
- **Key texts and authorities:** Shaiva Agamas, Tirumurai (Tamil hymns, including the Tevaram), Shiva Sutras; Abhinavagupta (Kashmir).
- **Free sources:** [Shaivam.org](https://shaivam.org/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Shaiva reading of the named school (Shaiva Siddhanta, Kashmir Shaivism, or Lingayat), citing its Agamas, hymns, or masters and noting where it differs from Vedanta.*

## Shakta

- **Distinctive beliefs:** the Goddess (Devi, Shakti, in forms such as Durga, Kali, Lalita) is ultimate reality and power; often tantric in practice.
- **Key texts and authorities:** Devi Mahatmya (from the Markandeya Purana), Devi Bhagavata Purana, Lalita Sahasranama, the Tantras.
- **Free sources:** [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/) (texts and glossaries; check each translation's source).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Shakta reading, citing the Devi Mahatmya by chapter and verse and the relevant Tantra or Purana, and note where it shares or differs from Shaiva tantra.*

## Smarta and Advaita Vedanta

- **Distinctive beliefs:** worship of five (or six) deities as forms of the one Brahman; Advaita Vedanta's non-dualism: liberation comes through knowledge (jnana) that the self is Brahman.
- **Key texts and authorities:** the Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, and Brahma Sutras (the three foundations of Vedanta) with Shankara's commentaries; the Shankaracharya mathas.
- **Free sources:** [Sringeri Sharada Peetham](https://www.sringeri.net/), [Gita Supersite](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/) (includes Shankara's Gita commentary).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Advaita Vedanta (Smarta) reading, citing the Upanishads, Gita, and Brahma Sutras with Shankara's commentary. Distinguish ultimate (paramarthika) from conventional (vyavaharika) truth.*

## Vishishtadvaita and Dvaita Vedanta

- **Vishishtadvaita (Ramanuja):** souls and world are real and form the "body" of God (Vishnu/Narayana); liberation through devotion and surrender (prapatti). Slot text: *Lead with Ramanuja's Vishishtadvaita, citing his Sri Bhashya and Gita commentary.*
- **Dvaita (Madhva):** God, souls, and matter are eternally distinct; souls depend wholly on God's grace. Free: [Dvaita.org](https://www.dvaita.org/). Slot text: *Lead with Madhva's Dvaita, citing his commentaries and the five eternal differences.*

## Primary vs comparison sources

- **Primary:** your sampradaya's scriptures and acharyas.
- **Comparison:** the other Vedanta schools' commentaries on the same verse (the Gita Supersite shows several side by side), academic Indology, and other religions' views.

## Points of dialogue

- Avatar and incarnation; grace and devotion in bhakti and in Christianity or Pure Land Buddhism.
- One God in many forms vs. strict monotheism; the self and ultimate reality.
- Rebirth and karma vs. a single life and judgment.
