## Overview

Muslims believe in one God (Allah), the prophethood of Muhammad as the final prophet, the Qur'an as God's word revealed to him, angels, earlier prophets and scriptures, the Day of Judgment, and divine decree. Practice centers on the Five Pillars (testimony of faith, prayer, almsgiving, fasting in Ramadan, pilgrimage to Mecca). Branches differ chiefly on leadership after Muhammad, which hadith are authoritative, and methods of law and theology.

## Branches at a glance

| | Sunni | Shia (Twelver) | Ibadi | Sufism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leadership after Muhammad | The first four caliphs, chosen by the community | Ali and a line of divinely appointed Imams | Leader chosen for piety; neither Sunni nor Shia | Not a separate sect; a mystical dimension within Sunni and Shia Islam |
| Hadith | The six canonical collections (Bukhari, Muslim, etc.) | Collections transmitted via the Imams (e.g. al-Kafi) | Own collection (al-Rabi' ibn Habib's Musnad) | Those of the Sufi's own branch |
| Law | Four madhhabs: Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali | Ja'fari school; living jurists (marja') | Ibadi jurisprudence | Follows a madhhab; adds spiritual path |
| Where | Most Muslims worldwide | Iran, Iraq, Bahrain, Lebanon, and elsewhere | Oman, parts of North and East Africa | Worldwide |

## Sunni Islam

- **Distinctive beliefs:** authority of the Qur'an and the Sunna as transmitted in the canonical hadith collections, the consensus of scholars, and analogical reasoning. Theological schools include Ash'ari, Maturidi, and Athari.
- **Key texts and authorities:** the Qur'an; Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, and the other four of the six books; founders of the madhhabs (Abu Hanifa, Malik, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad ibn Hanbal); al-Ghazali.
- **Free sources:** [Quran.com](https://quran.com/) (Arabic with many translations and tafsir), [Sunnah.com](https://sunnah.com/) (hadith collections with grading), [Altafsir.com](https://www.altafsir.com/) (classical commentaries).
- **The four madhhabs:** Hanafi (widest use; more room for reasoned opinion), Maliki (gives weight to the practice of Medina), Shafi'i (systematized legal theory), Hanbali (closest to the texts). They differ mainly on legal rulings and are mutually recognized.
- **Salafi:** a reform approach seeking to follow the "pious predecessors" (salaf) directly from the Qur'an and hadith, wary of later schools and practices; it overlaps with Hanbali and Athari thought. Some Muslims dispute Salafi critiques of traditional schools and Sufism.
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Sunni reading, naming the madhhab (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) or approach (e.g. Salafi) and theological school (Ash'ari, Maturidi, Athari). Cite the Qur'an by surah and verse, hadith by collection and number with its grading, and classical scholars by work.*

## Shia Islam

- **Distinctive beliefs:** after Muhammad, leadership belonged to Ali and his descendants, the Imams, who are divinely guided interpreters.
- **Twelver (Ithna 'Ashari):** the largest Shia branch; twelve Imams, the last in occultation and expected to return as the Mahdi. Ja'fari law; living senior jurists (marja') are followed. Texts: the Qur'an, *Nahj al-Balagha*, al-Kafi and the other "four books." Free: [Al-Islam.org](https://www.al-islam.org/). Slot text: *Lead with the Twelver Shia reading, citing the Qur'an, Nahj al-Balagha, and hadith from the four books with their chains, and name the marja' whose rulings you follow.*
- **Ismaili:** follow a different line of Imams after the sixth. The largest group, the Nizari Ismailis, follow a living Imam, the Aga Khan, and emphasize the inner (batin) meaning of scripture. Free: [The Ismaili](https://www.the.ismaili/). Slot text: *Lead with the Nizari Ismaili reading, distinguishing the outer (zahir) and inner (batin) meaning and citing the guidance of the Imam of the Time.*
- **Zaydi:** recognize Zayd ibn Ali as the fifth Imam; closer to Sunni positions in law and theology; mainly in Yemen. Slot text: *Lead with the Zaydi reading, noting where it is closer to Sunni positions than to Twelver ones.*

## Ibadi Islam

- **Distinctive beliefs:** descended from an early movement distinct from both Sunni and Shia; emphasizes piety-based leadership and moderate conduct toward other Muslims; majority tradition in Oman.
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Ibadi reading, citing Ibadi jurists and their hadith collection, and note where Ibadi positions differ from Sunni and Shia ones.*

## Sufism

- **Distinctive beliefs:** the inner, mystical path of drawing near to God through remembrance (dhikr), purification of the heart, and the guidance of a master within an order (tariqa), such as the Qadiriyya, Naqshbandiyya, Shadhiliyya, Chishtiyya, and Mevlevi. Sufis are found in both Sunni and Shia Islam. Some reformist Muslims criticize certain Sufi practices (e.g. saint veneration); Sufis defend them from the tradition.
- **Key texts and authorities:** al-Qushayri's *Risala*, al-Ghazali's *Revival of the Religious Sciences*, Rumi's *Masnavi*, Ibn Arabi; the order's own lineage.
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Sufi reading of the named order, grounding claims in the Qur'an and hadith and citing the order's masters. Distinguish devotional practice from legal rulings.*

## Ahmadiyya (status disputed)

The Ahmadiyya movement, founded by Mirza Ghulam Ahmad in 19th-century India, regards itself as Muslim. Most Sunni and Shia authorities do not accept it as Islamic because of its views on prophethood after Muhammad, and in some countries Ahmadis are legally barred from calling themselves Muslim. The movement has two branches (Ahmadiyya Muslim Community and the Lahore Ahmadiyya Movement), which differ on Ghulam Ahmad's status. Free: [Al Islam (Ahmadiyya Muslim Community)](https://www.alislam.org/). Slot text: *Lead with the Ahmadiyya reading of the named branch, citing its own sources, and note that most other Muslims dispute its status.*

## Primary vs comparison sources

- **Primary:** the Qur'an with your branch's hadith, law school, and scholars.
- **Comparison:** other branches' hadith and jurists, classical tafsir from several schools, academic Islamic studies, and Jewish and Christian sources where shared figures and stories are discussed.

## Points of dialogue

- The oneness of God (tawhid) and the Christian Trinity; Jesus (Isa) as prophet in Islam and as Son of God in Christianity.
- Revelation, preservation, and authority of scripture; the role of law (sharia and halakha compared).
- Prophets shared with Judaism and Christianity; grace, works, and judgment.
