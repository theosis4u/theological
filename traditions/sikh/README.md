## Overview

Sikhs follow the teachings of the ten Gurus, beginning with Guru Nanak (1469–1539), and the Guru Granth Sahib, the scripture regarded as the eternal living Guru. Core beliefs: one Creator (Ik Onkar), remembrance of God's Name (Naam), honest work, sharing with others, equality of all people, and service (seva). Initiated Sikhs (Khalsa) keep the Five Ks.

## Mainstream Sikhism (Khalsa and the Panth)

- **Distinctive beliefs:** the Guru Granth Sahib as eternal Guru; the Khalsa initiation; the Sikh Rehat Maryada (code of conduct) issued by the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee; the Akal Takht as the highest temporal seat.
- **Key texts and authorities:** Guru Granth Sahib; Dasam Granth (its authorship is debated among Sikhs); the writings of Bhai Gurdas; the Rehat Maryada.
- **Free sources:** [SearchGurbani](https://www.searchgurbani.com/), [SriGranth.org](https://www.srigranth.org/), [SikhiToTheMax](https://www.sikhitothemax.org/) (scripture with translations), [SikhiWiki](https://www.sikhiwiki.org/) (community encyclopedia; verify).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the mainstream Sikh reading, citing the Guru Granth Sahib by ang (page) and the Guru who wrote the passage, and the Sikh Rehat Maryada for practice.*

## Namdhari and Nirankari (status disputed)

Both movements began in the 19th century as reform movements and accept a continuing line of living Gurus, which mainstream Sikh bodies reject as contrary to the Guru Granth Sahib's status. The Sant Nirankari movement in particular has been in conflict with mainstream Sikh institutions. Slot text: *Lead with the named movement's own teaching and sources, and note that mainstream Sikh bodies dispute its status.*

## Primary vs comparison sources

- **Primary:** Guru Granth Sahib and the Rehat Maryada.
- **Comparison:** Hindu bhakti and Sufi poetry (some of which appears in the Guru Granth Sahib), academic Sikh studies, and other religions' views of God and grace.

## Points of dialogue

- One God and the Name; grace (nadar) compared with grace in other traditions.
- Rejection of caste and ritualism; relationship to Hinduism and Islam.
- Scripture as Guru; service and justice.
