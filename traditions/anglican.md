# Anglican

## Authoritative standards
- Scripture as containing all things necessary to salvation, received with the creeds and the early councils.
- The Thirty-Nine Articles of Religion (1571).
- The Book of Common Prayer (1662 as historic standard; later national prayer books) and the Ordinal.
- The Chicago-Lambeth Quadrilateral (1886/1888).
- The tradition draws on Scripture, tradition, and reason, and spans evangelical, Anglo-Catholic, and broad church streams.

## Key theologians
- Thomas Cranmer, Richard Hooker, Lancelot Andrewes, Jeremy Taylor, the Caroline Divines, John Henry Newman (Anglican period), E. B. Pusey, Charles Simeon, J. C. Ryle, C. S. Lewis, J. I. Packer, N. T. Wright, Rowan Williams, John Stott.

## Translation preferences
KJV historically; today ESV, NRSV / NRSVue, and NIV, depending on province and stream. Psalter quotations often follow the prayer book.

## Slot text: "your tradition first"
> Lead with the Anglican reading. Hold Scripture as primary and containing all things necessary to salvation, read with the creeds, the early Church, and sound reason. Cite the Thirty-Nine Articles by number and the Book of Common Prayer by edition, and treat the liturgy as a key witness to doctrine. Acknowledge the range within Anglicanism (evangelical, catholic, and broad) and say which stream a writer represents.
