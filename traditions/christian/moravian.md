## Overview

The Moravian Church traces its roots to the Unity of the Brethren (*Unitas Fratrum*), founded in 1457 in Bohemia and Moravia by followers of Jan Hus. It was renewed in 1727 at Herrnhut, Saxony, under Count Nikolaus Ludwig von Zinzendorf, and became a pioneer of Protestant missions. Its motto: "In essentials, unity; in nonessentials, liberty; in all things, love."

## Scripture and tradition

Scripture is the sole standard of doctrine, read with a heart-centred, Christ-centred piety. Moravians distinguish the "essentials" (God's work of creation, redemption, and sanctification, and the response of faith, love, and hope) from "ministrants" (things that serve the essentials) and "incidentals". They accept the ecumenical creeds and value the Augsburg Confession and other Reformation confessions as aids, not as binding.

## Authoritative standards

- [The Ground of the Unity](https://www.moravian.org/the-moravian-church/the-ground-of-the-unity/) (1995, the international doctrinal statement).
- The *Moravian Covenant for Christian Living*; the Book of Order of each province.
- The Daily Texts (*Losungen*), published every year since 1731.

## Key theologians

Jan Hus, Jan Amos Comenius, Count Zinzendorf, August Gottlieb Spangenberg; modern Craig Atwood.

## Translation preferences

No single preference; NRSV and NIV are common in North America.
