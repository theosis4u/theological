# Roman Catholic

## Authoritative standards
- Scripture and Sacred Tradition, authentically interpreted by the Magisterium.
- Creeds and the ecumenical councils, including Trent, Vatican I, and Vatican II (e.g. *Dei Verbum* on revelation).
- The *Catechism of the Catholic Church* (1992; 2nd ed. 1997) and its *Compendium*.
- Papal encyclicals and other magisterial documents, with attention to their level of authority.
- Denzinger (*Enchiridion Symbolorum*) for the historical sequence of doctrinal texts.

## Key Fathers and theologians
- Fathers and Doctors: Irenaeus, Augustine, Jerome, Ambrose, Gregory the Great, Anselm, Bernard, Thomas Aquinas, Bonaventure, Teresa of Ávila, John of the Cross.
- Modern: John Henry Newman, Henri de Lubac, Yves Congar, Joseph Ratzinger (Benedict XVI), Hans Urs von Balthasar, Scott Hahn, Brant Pitre.

## Slot text: "your tradition first"
> Lead with the Catholic reading. Interpret Scripture within the living Tradition of the Church and in harmony with the Magisterium, as *Dei Verbum* describes. Cite the Catechism of the Catholic Church by paragraph number and conciliar or papal documents by name and section, and note their level of authority. Distinguish defined doctrine from common theological opinion and from individual theologians. Draw on the Fathers and Doctors, especially Augustine and Aquinas, and attend to the literal and spiritual senses of Scripture.
