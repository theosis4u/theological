## Overview

The Restoration (Stone-Campbell) Movement began in the early 19th-century United States with Barton W. Stone, Thomas Campbell, and Alexander Campbell. It aimed to restore New Testament Christianity and unite Christians by setting aside creeds and denominational names ("Christians only, but not the only Christians"; "where the Scriptures speak, we speak; where the Scriptures are silent, we are silent").

(Other groups also call themselves "restorationist"; for Latter-day Saints, Jehovah's Witnesses, and others, see [nontrinitarian and restorationist offshoots](nontrinitarian-offshoots.md).)

## Branches

| Branch | Distinctives |
|---|---|
| **Churches of Christ** (a cappella) | No instruments in worship (from the silence of the New Testament); weekly Lord's Supper; congregational autonomy; baptism by immersion for the forgiveness of sins |
| **Christian Churches / Churches of Christ** (independent) | Instruments allowed; evangelical; congregational |
| **Christian Church (Disciples of Christ)** | A mainline denomination; open communion; progressive theology in many places; ecumenical |

## Scripture and tradition

The New Testament is the pattern for the church; human creeds are not tests of fellowship. Interpretation stresses "command, example, and necessary inference" (especially in Churches of Christ). The Disciples affirm Scripture but read it with more openness to historical criticism.

## Authoritative standards

- Scripture alone; no binding creed.
- Founding documents: Thomas Campbell's *Declaration and Address* (1809); *The Last Will and Testament of the Springfield Presbytery* (1804).
- For Disciples, the *Design for the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ)*.

## Key theologians

Barton W. Stone, Thomas and Alexander Campbell, Walter Scott, David Lipscomb; scholars Leroy Garrett, Richard Hughes, Douglas Foster, Everett Ferguson (*The Church of Christ*).
