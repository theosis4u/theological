## What "evangelical" means

"Evangelical" names a **movement that runs across many Protestant traditions**, not a single tradition with one set of standards. Two people who both say "I'm evangelical" may disagree on baptism, predestination, spiritual gifts, and church government. So this page is not a pack. Use it to find the pack that fits, and then set that pack's primary sources.

(In German and some European usage, *evangelisch* simply means "Protestant", often Lutheran. That is a different sense.)

## The Bebbington quadrilateral

The historian David Bebbington (*Evangelicalism in Modern Britain*, 1989) described four marks that have become the most widely used working definition:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Conversionism** | Lives need to be changed by personal conversion (being "born again") |
| **Biblicism** | A particular regard for the Bible as the supreme authority |
| **Crucicentrism** | A stress on Christ's sacrifice on the cross |
| **Activism** | The gospel expressed in effort, especially evangelism and mission |

Other scholars (for example Mark Noll, Thomas Kidd, and Timothy Larsen) refine or debate this definition, but it is the common starting point.

## Statements that describe the movement

These are cross-tradition statements, not confessions that replace a church's own standards:
- [Lausanne Covenant (1974)](https://www.lausanne.org/content/covenant/lausanne-covenant), drafted chiefly by John Stott.
- [National Association of Evangelicals: Statement of Faith](https://www.nae.org/statement-of-faith/) and [What is an evangelical?](https://www.nae.org/what-is-an-evangelical/) (US).
- [World Evangelical Alliance](https://worldea.org/) (global).

## Where they differ

| Question | Range of evangelical views |
|---|---|
| **Baptism** | Infant baptism as a covenant sign (Reformed, Anglican, Methodist, Lutheran) vs. believer's baptism only (Baptist, most Pentecostal and nondenominational); immersion vs. other modes; sacrament vs. ordinance |
| **Predestination** | Calvinist (unconditional election) vs. Arminian/Wesleyan (conditional election, prevenient grace), with many in between |
| **Spiritual gifts** | Continuationist (Pentecostal, charismatic) vs. cessationist (many Reformed and Baptist) vs. "open but cautious" |
| **Church government** | Episcopal (bishops: Anglican, Methodist), presbyterian (elders and courts), congregational (each congregation self-governing: Baptist, most nondenominational) |
| **End times** | Premillennial (dispensational or historic), amillennial, postmillennial |
| **Lord's Supper** | Spiritual presence (Reformed), memorial (many Baptist and nondenominational), and others |

## US cultural and political usage

In the United States, "evangelical" is also used as a **demographic and political category**, especially in polling and journalism, often tied to voting patterns. Surveys differ in whether they define evangelicals by self-identification, by denomination, or by belief (for example, the NAE and LifeWay Research belief-based definition). Some Christians who hold the four marks avoid the word because of these associations; others embrace it. This page takes no side; for study, ask about beliefs and church standards rather than the label.

## How to pick a real pack

1. **Name the church or denomination** you belong to, or the one whose teaching you hold.
2. **Ask the decisive questions:** How do you baptize, and whom? Calvinist, Arminian, or unsure? Do you expect the gifts of the Spirit (tongues, prophecy, healing) today? Who governs the church?
3. **Pick the matching pack** from the table above, and set its standards as your primaries.
4. **If the church is independent,** use the [nondenominational pack](nondenominational.md): the church's own **statement of faith** is the primary source.
