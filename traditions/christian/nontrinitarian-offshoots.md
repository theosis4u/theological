## Overview

This page covers groups that arose from Christianity, regard themselves as Christian (or, for some Unitarian Universalists, as a broader faith), and reject or redefine the doctrine of the Trinity as defined at Nicaea (325) and Constantinople (381), or add new scripture or revelation. **Their status as Christian is disputed.**

- **How each group sees itself:** most see themselves as restoring original Christianity that later churches lost.
- **Who disputes it and why:** the Catholic, Orthodox, and most Protestant churches treat the Nicene doctrine of the Trinity (and, for some, the closed canon) as defining Christian orthodoxy, and many do not recognize these groups' baptisms.

This page describes, without settling the dispute. Use each group's own sources for its views, and the other packs for responses.

| Group | Self-understanding | View of God | Scripture and other authorities |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Latter-day Saints** | The restored Church of Jesus Christ | Father, Son, and Holy Ghost as three distinct beings, one in purpose | Bible, Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, Pearl of Great Price; living prophets |
| **Jehovah's Witnesses** | True Christians restoring first-century faith | Jehovah alone is almighty God; Jesus is God's Son, created; the holy spirit is God's active force | Bible (*New World Translation*); the Governing Body's publications |
| **Oneness Pentecostals** | Apostolic Pentecostals | One God who reveals himself as Father, Son, and Spirit (modes or manifestations), not three persons | Bible; baptism in Jesus' name (Acts 2:38) |
| **Unitarians (historic)** | Reformed Christians who reject the Trinity | God is one person; Jesus is the Son, not God the Son | Bible, reason |
| **Unitarian Universalists** | A liberal, creedless religion of many sources | Varies: theist, humanist, and others | No required scripture; the UUA principles and sources |
| **Christian Science** | A rediscovery of Jesus' healing Christianity | God as divine Principle, Mind, Spirit, Love; matter and evil unreal | Bible and Mary Baker Eddy, *Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures* |

## Latter-day Saints

(Moved here from [Other traditions](../other/README.md).)
- **Scripture and tradition:** the Bible "as far as it is translated correctly" plus the Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, and Pearl of Great Price; continuing revelation through living prophets and apostles.
- **Branches:** The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (largest); Community of Christ (formerly RLDS, which has moved toward mainline Protestant views); smaller groups.
- **Disputed status:** Latter-day Saints regard themselves as Christians. Most Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant churches do not consider Latter-day Saint teaching on the nature of God and the Trinity to be within historic Christian orthodoxy, and do not recognize its baptism.
- **Free sources:** [Scriptures (The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints)](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures); [Community of Christ](https://www.cofchrist.org/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Latter-day Saint reading, citing the Book of Mormon and Doctrine and Covenants by book, chapter, and verse and church leaders' teachings, and note where other Christian traditions disagree, from their own sources.*

## Jehovah's Witnesses

- **Scripture and tradition:** the Bible alone, as read in the *New World Translation* and explained by the Governing Body; tradition and creeds rejected as later corruptions.
- **Distinctives:** God's name Jehovah; Jesus as the first creation and Michael the archangel; no immortal soul; a heavenly class of 144,000 and an earthly hope for others; no blood transfusions; political neutrality.
- **Disputed status:** Witnesses call themselves Christians but reject the Trinity and the label "Protestant". Other churches dispute their Christology (Arian-like) and the *New World Translation*'s rendering of texts such as John 1:1.
- **Free sources:** [JW.org Online Bible](https://www.jw.org/en/library/bible/); [Watchtower Online Library](https://wol.jw.org/en/).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Jehovah's Witness reading, citing the New World Translation and Watchtower publications by title and date, and note where other Christians disagree, from their own sources.*

## Oneness Pentecostals

- **Scripture and tradition:** the Bible alone; Pentecostal experience; rejection of "three persons" as unbiblical language.
- **Distinctives:** baptism in the name of Jesus only; many teach that speaking in tongues is the initial sign of Spirit baptism, necessary for salvation; holiness standards.
- **Disputed status:** Oneness believers see themselves as apostolic Christians. Trinitarian Pentecostals (the Assemblies of God separated from them in 1916) and other churches regard Oneness theology as modalism.
- **Free sources:** [United Pentecostal Church International: Our beliefs](https://www.upci.org/about/our-beliefs).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Oneness Pentecostal reading, citing the UPCI Articles of Faith and Scripture, especially Acts 2:38, and note where Trinitarian Christians disagree, from their own sources.*

## Unitarians (historic) and Unitarian Universalists

- **Historic Unitarians** (Socinians in Poland, English and American Unitarians such as Joseph Priestley and William Ellery Channing) were Bible-centred Christians who held that God is one person. Small Unitarian Christian groups continue.
- **Unitarian Universalists** (UUA, formed 1961) are a creedless faith drawing on many sources: Christian, Jewish, humanist, earth-centred, and others. Many UUs do not identify as Christian; some do (for example the Unitarian Christian Association).
- **Disputed status:** Trinitarian churches regard Unitarian theology as outside orthodoxy; UUs generally do not claim Christian orthodoxy and describe their faith on its own terms.
- **Free sources:** [UUA: Our faith](https://www.uua.org/beliefs). Small Unitarian Christian groups have their own sites; check them in a browser.
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Unitarian reading for my branch (historic Unitarian Christian or Unitarian Universalist), citing its own writers and statements, and note where Trinitarian Christians disagree, from their own sources.*

## Christian Science

- **Scripture and tradition:** the Bible and *Science and Health* as the church's "pastor"; Mary Baker Eddy (1821–1910) founded the Church of Christ, Scientist in 1879.
- **Distinctives:** God as divine Mind; matter, sin, sickness, and death as unreal; healing through prayer and spiritual understanding.
- **Disputed status:** Christian Scientists regard themselves as Christians. Other churches dispute its views of God, Christ, sin, and the material world.
- **Free sources:** [Christian Science](https://www.christianscience.com/); [Science and Health](https://www.christianscience.com/the-christian-science-pastor/science-and-health).
- **Slot text:** *Lead with the Christian Science reading, citing Science and Health by page and line and the Bible, and note where other Christians disagree, from their own sources.*
