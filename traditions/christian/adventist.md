## Overview

The Seventh-day Adventist Church grew out of the Millerite movement in the 1840s United States and was organized in 1863. Founders include Ellen G. White, James White, and Joseph Bates. It keeps the seventh-day Sabbath, expects the soon, literal second coming of Christ, and stresses health and wholeness.

## Scripture and tradition

Adventists hold *sola scriptura*: Scripture is the only rule of faith. Ellen G. White is regarded as having a genuine prophetic gift; her writings speak with prophetic authority but point to Scripture and are not added to the canon (Fundamental Belief 18, "The Gift of Prophecy"). How her authority relates to Scripture is debated inside and outside the church.

## Distinctives

- Sabbath (Friday sunset to Saturday sunset).
- The heavenly sanctuary and the investigative (pre-advent) judgment since 1844.
- Conditional immortality (the dead sleep until the resurrection; the wicked are destroyed, not tormented forever).
- Believer's baptism by immersion; health message (many are vegetarian).

## Disputed status

Adventists see themselves as Protestant Christians. Most Protestant bodies today regard the church as Trinitarian and evangelical in core beliefs, after dialogues in the 1950s (*Questions on Doctrine*, 1957; Walter Martin's reassessment). Some critics still dispute the investigative judgment and the role of Ellen White. Groups that left (for example some Sabbath-keeping or reform movements) are distinct.

## Authoritative standards

- The *28 Fundamental Beliefs* (on adventist.org, which blocks automated checks; loads in a browser).
- *Seventh-day Adventists Believe* (official exposition).
- Ellen G. White, e.g. *The Great Controversy*, *Steps to Christ*, *The Desire of Ages*.

## Key theologians

Ellen G. White, Uriah Smith, J. N. Andrews; modern Richard Rice, George Knight, Fernando Canale, Jon Paulien.
