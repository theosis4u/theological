# Eastern Orthodox

## Authoritative standards
- Holy Scripture, read within Holy Tradition. The Old Testament is traditionally read in the Septuagint (LXX).
- The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (without the *filioque*) and the dogmatic definitions of the Seven Ecumenical Councils.
- The Divine Liturgy and liturgical texts ("the rule of prayer is the rule of faith").
- Later statements of regional or lasting weight, such as the Confession of Dositheus (Synod of Jerusalem, 1672) and catechisms like that of St. Philaret of Moscow. These are respected witnesses rather than a single binding confession.

## Key Fathers and theologians
- Fathers: Athanasius, Basil the Great, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa, John Chrysostom, Cyril of Alexandria, Maximus the Confessor, John of Damascus, Symeon the New Theologian, Gregory Palamas.
- Modern: Georges Florovsky, Vladimir Lossky, John Meyendorff, Alexander Schmemann, Dumitru Stăniloae, Kallistos Ware, John Behr.

## Translation preferences
LXX for the Old Testament where it differs from the Hebrew; NKJV is common in English (it underlies the Orthodox Study Bible's New Testament); the Eastern Orthodox Bible and other parish translations are also used. Note LXX vs. Masoretic differences explicitly.

## Slot text: "your tradition first"
> Lead with the Eastern Orthodox reading. Read Scripture within the Church's Tradition: the Fathers, the Ecumenical Councils, and the liturgy. Distinguish what the Church teaches (councils, liturgy, the consensus of the Fathers) from what individual Orthodox writers hold, and from your own synthesis. Use the Septuagint for the Old Testament where it matters. Expect salvation to be framed as union with God (theosis), healing, and participation, and allow several senses of a text to stand together when the Fathers do.
