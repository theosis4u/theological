## Overview

This is a **17th-century movement of prayer and spiritual renewal**, mostly within Roman Catholicism, not a church. It stressed interior, passive prayer, abandonment to God, and "pure love" (love of God without regard to reward). Its readers today include Catholics, Quakers, Pietists, Methodists, and many evangelical and charismatic Christians.

## Figures

| Figure | Key work | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Miguel de Molinos** (1628–1696) | *The Spiritual Guide* (1675) | Condemned by Pope Innocent XI in *Coelestis Pastor* (1687); imprisoned |
| **Madame Jeanne Guyon** (1648–1717) | *A Short and Easy Method of Prayer* (1685) | Imprisoned; her views examined at Issy (1695) |
| **François Fénelon** (1651–1715), Archbishop of Cambrai | *Maxims of the Saints* (1697) | Opposed by Bossuet; some propositions condemned by Innocent XII (1699); Fénelon submitted |

## Disputed status

The Roman Catholic Church condemned specific Quietist propositions (for example, that the soul should become wholly passive and give up acts and the desire for salvation). Catholic mystics such as Teresa of Ávila and John of the Cross remain fully approved, and Catholic writers distinguish their teaching from condemned Quietism. Supporters argue that Guyon and Fénelon were misread or caught in church politics. Protestants who read Guyon and Fénelon (John Wesley published extracts) usually value their devotion while not adopting every claim.

## Scripture and tradition

These writers stayed within Catholic teaching on Scripture and tradition, but stressed inner experience of God in prayer over method and outward works. Readers from other traditions read them through their own standards.

## Pietism note

German **Pietism** (Philipp Jakob Spener's *Pia Desideria*, 1675; August Hermann Francke at Halle) was a parallel Lutheran renewal: heart religion, small groups, Bible study, and practical holiness. It is not Quietism, but both emphasize inward devotion; Pietism shaped the Moravians, the Brethren, and Methodism.

## Key theologians

Molinos, Guyon, Fénelon, Bossuet (opponent), Pierre Poiret (editor); for Pietism, Spener, Francke, Johann Arndt (*True Christianity*); scholars Bernard McGinn (history of Christian mysticism).
