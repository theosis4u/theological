## Overview

The Anabaptists ("re-baptizers") arose in the 1520s in Switzerland, South Germany, and the Netherlands, in the Radical Reformation. They practise believer's baptism, stress discipleship (*Nachfolge*), the church as a voluntary, disciplined community separate from the state, and (for most) nonresistance or peace.

## Branches

| Branch | Origin | Distinctives |
|---|---|---|
| **Mennonite** | Menno Simons (Dutch, 1530s–50s) | Wide range from conservative to progressive; peace churches; Mennonite Central Committee |
| **Amish** | Jakob Ammann (1693 split from Swiss Brethren) | *Ordnung* (community rules), plain dress, limits on technology, shunning (*Meidung*) |
| **Hutterite** | Jakob Hutter (Moravia, 1530s) | Community of goods; colony life |
| **Brethren** | Schwarzenau Brethren (1708, Anabaptist and Pietist roots) | Church of the Brethren, Brethren in Christ, and others; trine immersion, love feast (distinct from the Plymouth Brethren) |

## Scripture and tradition

Scripture, especially the New Testament and the Sermon on the Mount, read in community, is the authority. Anabaptists stress obedience over doctrine alone and the gathered church's discernment ("the hermeneutic community"). Confessions are statements of the community's understanding, not creeds over Scripture.

## Authoritative standards

- The Schleitheim Confession (1527) and the Dordrecht Confession (1632) (the latter is in Schaff, *Creeds of Christendom*, Vol. III).
- [Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective (1995)](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/).
- Menno Simons, *Foundation of Christian Doctrine*; the *Martyrs Mirror* (1660).
- Each conference's or church's statement; for the Amish, the local *Ordnung* (unwritten).

## Key theologians

Conrad Grebel, Michael Sattler, Balthasar Hubmaier, Pilgram Marpeck, Menno Simons, Dirk Philips; modern Harold Bender ("The Anabaptist Vision"), John Howard Yoder, C. Arnold Snyder, Donald Kraybill (on the Amish).

## Translation preferences

KJV and the German Luther Bible among conservative groups; NRSV, NIV, and others among progressive Mennonites.

## Slot text: "your tradition first"

> Lead with the Anabaptist reading for my branch (Mennonite, Amish, Hutterite, or Brethren, and my conference). Cite the Schleitheim and Dordrecht Confessions, the Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, and Menno Simons by work and section.
