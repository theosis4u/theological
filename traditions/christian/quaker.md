## Overview

The Religious Society of Friends began in 17th-century England with George Fox and others. Friends speak of the "Inward Light" or "that of God in everyone", and of waiting on the Spirit in worship. They have long-standing testimonies (often summarized as simplicity, peace, integrity, community, equality, and stewardship).

## Branches

| Branch | Worship | Typical theology | Bodies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unprogrammed** | Silent waiting worship; anyone may speak as led | Liberal to Christ-centred; some universalist or nontheist Friends | Friends General Conference (US), Britain Yearly Meeting, many others |
| **Programmed** | Pastors, hymns, sermons, with some open worship | Christ-centred, often evangelical | Friends United Meeting |
| **Evangelical Friends** | Programmed, church-like services | Evangelical, Holiness-influenced | Evangelical Friends Church International |
| **Conservative Friends** | Unprogrammed, traditional | Christ-centred, traditional plainness | Small yearly meetings (e.g. Ohio) |

## Scripture and tradition

Early Friends held that the same Spirit who inspired Scripture speaks directly today, so Scripture is a secondary rule, tested by and testing the Spirit's leading (Robert Barclay, *Apology*, Proposition 3). Evangelical and programmed Friends usually give Scripture final authority in practice; many liberal unprogrammed Friends give more weight to continuing revelation and the meeting's discernment. Friends have no creeds; each yearly meeting's *Faith and Practice* gathers testimonies and advice.

## Authoritative standards

- Each yearly meeting's *Faith and Practice* (the British version is [Quaker faith & practice](https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/)).
- George Fox's *Journal*; Robert Barclay's *Apology for the True Christian Divinity* (1676).
- For evangelical Friends, the Richmond Declaration of Faith (1887) and their church's statement.

## Key theologians

George Fox, Margaret Fell, Robert Barclay, William Penn, Isaac Penington, John Woolman, Elias Hicks, Rufus Jones, Thomas Kelly, Howard Brinton; scholars Douglas Gwyn, Rosemary Moore, Ben Pink Dandelion.
