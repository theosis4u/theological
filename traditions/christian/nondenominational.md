## Overview

Nondenominational (independent) churches are not part of a denomination. Most are evangelical in belief, congregational in government, and practice believer's baptism, but this varies widely. Some are charismatic, some Calvinist, some Arminian; some belong to loose networks (for example Calvary Chapel, Vineyard, Acts 29, or various church-planting networks) that are denominations in all but name.

## Scripture and tradition

Most hold Scripture as the sole final authority (often "inerrant" or "infallible") and are wary of tradition as an authority, though in practice they inherit a great deal from Baptist, Pentecostal, Reformed, or revivalist streams. Many affirm the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds; some do not use creeds at all.

## Authoritative standards

- **Primary source: the church's own statement of faith** (often on its website, under "What we believe" or "Beliefs"). Quote it by section.
- The network's statement, if the church belongs to one.
- Commonly adopted summary statements, such as the [NAE Statement of Faith](https://www.nae.org/statement-of-faith/) or the [Lausanne Covenant](https://www.lausanne.org/content/covenant/lausanne-covenant).
- Where the statement is silent, ask the user (or the church's elders) rather than assuming a tradition.

## Key theologians

There is no shared canon. Ask which teachers the church and user follow. Writers often read in these churches include John Stott, Wayne Grudem (*Systematic Theology*), J. I. Packer, Tim Keller, Chuck Smith (Calvary Chapel), John Wimber (Vineyard), and popular preachers; note each writer's own tradition.
